It's parking ions,

The shape of a charge

Power is not a number.
It's a curve that falls away under you.

A charger advertises its ceiling. The battery decides what it will actually accept, and that decision changes every second as the anode fills. The grey envelope is what the pack will take; the coloured area is what you actually get.

PHASE 01 · CONSTANT CURRENT

Push as hard as it will take

Empty lattice, low resistance, plenty of free sites. Current is capped only by the charger, the cable, or the pack's own limit. This is the part that fits on a billboard.

PHASE 02 · TAPER

Cell voltage hits its ceiling

Each cell approaches roughly 4.2 V. To avoid going past it, the charger holds voltage steady and lets current fall. Power drops even though the charger has plenty in reserve.

PHASE 03 · CONSTANT VOLTAGE

The long crawl to full

Free sites are scarce. Push too hard now and lithium plates as metal on the anode surface instead of slotting in — permanent capacity loss. So the current trickles, and the last 20% can take as long as the first 70%.

Cell → module → pack

A truck is not a big car battery.
It's a great many car battery cells.

Every cell in the pack is doing the same thing at the same time. Scale is just repetition — which is why a truck needs a megawatt to charge in the same minutes a car needs 350 kW.

Thermal limits

Cold batteries won't take it.
Hot batteries aren't allowed to.

Below about 10 °C the electrolyte is sluggish and ions move too slowly to be forced in safely, so the pack throttles itself hard. Above about 40 °C, degradation accelerates and it throttles again. Fast charging sits in a narrow window — and generates the very heat that threatens it.
